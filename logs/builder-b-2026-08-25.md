## 2026-08-25T02:48:33Z — LAI-220 — a rate-limited sign-in blamed the password

A peer session sent a correction: LAI-219 claimed there is no brake on repeated
failed sign-ins, and they had measured `429`s. Checking it found that **we were
both right, under different `NODE_ENV`s** — and turned up a regression I had
shipped in LAI-078 an hour earlier.

### Establishing the facts before correcting anything

Their report and my measurement disagreed, so I reproduced rather than deferring.
`NODE_ENV=production` on my instance: attempts 1–3 `401`, 4–8 `429`. Exactly
what they saw. My original measurement had `NODE_ENV` unset — development.

**LAI-096 already had the answer in a table nobody had connected to this:**
better-auth's own rate limiting is `off / on / off` across development,
production and test.

But their attribution was wrong, and it mattered. They said it was *"our own
IP-based limiter"*. It is not ours, and ours is not IP-based:

- the `429` carried `x-ratelimit-remaining: 595` — **our** limiter had not fired
- its body was `"Too many requests. Please try again later."` with
  `details.message` and `x-retry-after: 10`; ours sends `"Too many requests"`
  with `retry_after_seconds` and a standard `Retry-After`
- `rate-limit.ts` documents *why* ours is not per-IP: that needs a trusted-proxy
  config Laika does not have, so anonymous requests share one bucket

So the brake is better-auth's, it is keyed on the caller, and **it does not exist
unless the operator sets `NODE_ENV=production`**. Neither of us had said that,
and it is the part a self-hoster needs to know.

### The regression that fell out of checking

`api/auth.ts` wraps **any** error with a `status` into `SignInError`. LAI-078
then mapped every `SignInError` to `rejected`. So a `429` rendered *"Email or
password is wrong."*

Measured: five rapid failures, then the **correct** password → *"Email or
password is wrong."* The user retries with credentials that are fine, stays
limited, and concludes they have forgotten their password. Before LAI-078 this
path showed the server's own message and was right.

**I made it worse by collapsing two failures into one boolean.** The lesson is
narrow and worth keeping: a boolean is the right shape for *"is this the reader's
fault"* only if every path that sets it has actually been checked. I checked the
`401` path and assumed the rest.

Fixed by carrying the status and branching on `401` alone.

### Protocol

I was holding LAI-086, unstarted. Rather than fix this off the books I released
it untouched, filed **LAI-220** as p1, claimed it, and fixed it. One task at a
time, and the work still traces to a task file.

### Also

- Removed `api/auth.ts` from `WEB_NO_MIRROR_REQUIRED`. Its stated reason was
  "thin better-auth boundary" — true while it only forwarded a failure, false
  once a caller had to ask why. **An exemption's justification can expire
  without the exemption noticing.**
- Corrected the `LoginScreen` comment and LAI-219's premise and title, to what I
  measured rather than to what the correction said.
- `pnpm lint` caught an unnecessary `as unknown as typeof fetch` in the new test.

### The durable lesson from LAI-220, stated generally

*Only reachable in production, which is why it survived review.*

better-auth's rate limiting is **off in development and test, on in production**
(LAI-096's measured table). So the 429 path — and the bug where it rendered as
"Email or password is wrong" — could not occur in any dev loop, in any test run,
or in either session's normal checking. It was not missed through carelessness;
it was **unreachable by the way we look**.

The general form, which is worth more than the auth case:

> **Anything gated on `NODE_ENV` is invisible to how we normally check.** A dev
> server and a test runner are both *not production*, so a behaviour that only
> exists in production has no witness. Both of the ways we usually establish
> truth here — run it locally, write a test — are blind to it by construction.

Two things follow that I intend to keep doing:

1. When behaviour depends on the environment, **say which environment the
   measurement was taken in.** My original LAI-078 note said "this instance has
   no lockout" — true, and useless, because it omitted that the instance was in
   development. A measurement without its conditions is an anecdote.
2. `grep` for `NODE_ENV` / `isTest()` / `import.meta.env` in the area being
   changed before claiming anything about its runtime behaviour. LAI-096 had
   already written the whole table down; neither session connected it, because
   neither went looking.

The same shape as the exemption that expired (`api/auth.ts` was a "thin
boundary" until it wasn't) and the dead CSS that broke its replacement: **a
justification recorded once, still true when written, and no longer true when
relied upon.** Nothing fails when one of these goes stale — that is what makes
them worth writing down rather than trusting to memory.
