**Decisions**
- Sprint = a band across the top of its days (neutral; calm blue for the running
  one) instead of a cell tint. Days stay neutral, so a background comparison
  between a sprint day and an ordinary weekday is the test.
- Priority is a label (weight carries it), not a red/orange tint: the audit's
  point was that red must mean overdue. Agent mark moved from pink to accent
  for the same reason.
- Chips are two lines (key + title / status + priority + Overdue), the only
  layout that fits all four in a 1440 month cell without overflow.
- Chips inside the grid are `tabIndex -1`; the day is the roving stop. Enter on
  a one-task day opens the task; on a busier day it opens the day list. Opening
  from the list focuses the day first, so the drawer (which restores focus to
  whatever had it at open, `TaskDrawer.tsx:81`) returns the reader to the day.
- The day list is fixed-positioned rather than inside the cell, because the
  card clips its overflow. It starts `visibility: hidden` until placed, and
  hidden elements refuse focus, so focus moves in an effect after placement
  (found by the test).
- Agenda breakpoint 640px (not My day's 900px): between the two the grid still
  reads with horizontal scroll from a 46rem floor.
- Browser tests pin the clock (`page.clock.setFixedTime`) and emulate reduced
  motion: overdue needs a day before today inside the grid, and colours read
  straight after a theme switch caught a chip mid-transition.
