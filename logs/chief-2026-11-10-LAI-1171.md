**Measurement (the answer to the lead's question 1).** A throwaway probe
(deleted) opened the fixture at eight pinned dates, both zooms, 1366px. At
Quarters the window ended on the quarter boundary a week past the last sprint
(31 Dec on 10 Nov; 30 Sep on 15 Aug) while the pill, ≈145px ≈ ±21 days at
3.5px/day, blanked the one month label left to its right (`Dec`, `Sep`). On
9 Nov `Dec` was 22 days on and just clear — hence green yesterday, red today.
Dates reaching into the next quarter (15 Dec, 31 Dec, 1 Jan, 20 Mar) were
fine. **(a): the product drew no label right of today because the window
ended before the next labelled month; the assertion's premise was sound.**

**Decisions.**
- Fix in the derive layer as a reach past *today*, not as a wider air around
  sprints: the air is about bars not touching the edge; this is about the
  header having a label beside the pill, and it only bites when the sprints
  are behind today.
- The Quarters reach is "the first of the month after next" rather than a
  pill-width computation: `chartWindow` knows no pixels, and the next month's
  first is clear of the pill only while today is on or before the ~8th. The
  cost is up to one extra quarter early in a month (10 Aug → 31 Dec, was
  30 Sept); the header is drawn only for the stretch in view (LAI-721), so
  this is axis length, not DOM. Said in the task file for the reviewer.
- Two weeks at Weeks and Months: a Monday at ≥ 8 days (clear of a ±7-day
  pill) always falls within 14.
- The pinned dates are literals with the clock pinned to them, the shape
  CONVENTIONS allows (*"if a test needs a fixed instant, pin `now` too"*) and
  the Auckland test in the same file already uses.

**Discoveries.**
- A sprint-timeline test on the real clock is calendar-dependent through
  `chartWindow`'s snapping even when every fixture date is `today ± n`: the
  *shape* of the window changes with where today sits in the quarter.
- Unpinning the clock is not a usable mutation once the product is fixed —
  the real-clock run passes today too. The mutation that proves the test is
  the product revert.
